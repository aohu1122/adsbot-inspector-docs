ChatGPT Ads for Small Business: 5 First-Week Mistakes

You no longer need an agency retainer or a five-figure minimum to advertise in ChatGPT — self-serve is open with no spend floor. But a small budget makes mistakes proportionally more expensive. These five account for most of the wasted spend we see in week one.

Mistake 1: Submitting a page the crawler can't see

Your site works fine in a browser, so you assume it works for OpenAI's reviewer. But if your CDN blocks the OAI-AdsBot user-agent, or your page is JavaScript-only, the crawler sees a blocked page or a blank shell. The ad gets stuck in review or dies silently — while you wait days blaming the platform. Do instead: simulate the crawl before your first submission; the Cloudflare/WAF guide covers the most common blocker and its fix.

Mistake 2: Leaving context hints empty (or stuffing them)

Context hints tell OpenAI what your page is about; they feed targeting and relevance scoring. Two failure modes: leaving them blank (the system guesses from whatever the crawler managed to read) or pasting fifty keywords (diluted signal, possible mismatch flags). Do instead: 5–10 phrases that literally describe your page's offer — the same language as your H1 and ad copy. A good scanning tool will propose them from your actual page content.

Mistake 3: Copy that pattern-matches "misleading"

"Guaranteed results", "lose 10 pounds in a week", "only 3 spots left", "as seen on Forbes" (when you weren't) — claim patterns like these are flagged in review even when your business is honest. Small businesses write them out of enthusiasm, not fraud. Do instead: concrete, provable statements ("240+ local businesses served since 2019"). Full list of tripwires in the 7 rejection reasons.

Mistake 4: Budget copied from your Google Ads playbook

ChatGPT Ads in 2026 prices roughly at $25–60 CPM / $3–5 CPC — but it's a conversational surface, not search: intent signals differ, volume per campaign is lower, and optimization data accumulates slower. Porting a search-style daily budget and expecting search-style volume leads to either starved delivery or panicked overbidding in week one. Do instead: define a fixed test budget you're comfortable spending 3–5 times before judging, and judge against your own written success metric (see mistake 5).

Mistake 5: Renewing on vibes

Week one ends, the dashboard shows impressions and some clicks, and the subscription renews because "it seems to be working". Conversational channels make vanity metrics feel warm. Do instead: before launch, write one line: "I will keep spending only if [event] happens ≥ N times per week by day 14." Tie the event to money — trials, calls, checkouts — and let the line decide.
